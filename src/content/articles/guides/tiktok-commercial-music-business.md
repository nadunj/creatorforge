Choosing a trending song for a product video involves more than finding a good beat. A sound available for ordinary TikTok use may not be licensed for a post promoting a business.

If you publish shop updates, service demonstrations, or sponsored videos, make music selection part of your production plan before finishing the edit.

## Start with what the post promotes

TikTok recommends using its Commercial Music Library when content promotes a brand, product, or service. It says the licenses it holds for music outside that library do not cover commercial use in content.

If you use original sound or music outside the library with the commercial content disclosure enabled, TikTok requires its Music Usage Confirmation. That confirmation concerns whether copyrighted music is absent or the necessary licenses have been obtained. [Source: TikTok commercial music guidance](https://support.tiktok.com/en/business-and-creator/creator-and-business-accounts/commercial-use-of-music-on-tiktok).

Do not decide which rights to check solely from the account label. Start by describing the purpose of the actual post. "A customer-facing demonstration of our new product" is more useful than "a video on my personal account."

## Find a suitable commercial track

TikTok's business guidance explains how to access Commercial Sounds and filter the library by region, usable placements, mood, genre, and duration. Business accounts see the commercial library when adding sounds, while personal-account users can select Commercial Sounds. Verify the available track and placement for the region involved. [Source: TikTok Commercial Music Library instructions](https://ads.tiktok.com/resources/help/article/how-to-use-the-commercial-music-library?lang=en-GB).

Begin with the message rather than the genre. A product demonstration needs room for explanation; a short packaging montage may need a more rhythmic track. Compare a few options with the narration playing, not in isolation.

Listen to the opening, the busiest section, and the ending. A track that works for the first five seconds may become distracting when the speaker explains a price or an important limitation.

## Keep a clean master for other platforms

Save an export containing your dialogue and essential sound effects without the platform-selected music. Then make the TikTok version from that master. This is a practical editing recommendation that makes it easier to choose a different soundtrack for another destination.

TikTok's Commercial Music Library terms do not grant commercial-use rights outside TikTok, apart from the permitted sharing features. Posting the music on another platform requires separate permission and licensing from the rights holders. Keep that distinction in mind when making a cross-platform export. [Source: TikTok Commercial Music Library terms](https://www.tiktok.com/legal/page/global/commercial-music-library-user-terms/en).

## Make the music decision easy to trace

Keep a simple record with the video: track title, library or supplier, date selected, intended platforms, campaign region, and any required credit. Save your license documentation alongside it.

If a client changes a post into an advertisement or adds another publishing destination, revisit the music choice before delivery. Ask for those uses in the initial brief so the soundtrack can be selected once with the whole job in mind.

A repeatable music workflow begins with the intended commercial use, then checks the track, placement, and destination. The song should support the message and the permissions should support the publication.
