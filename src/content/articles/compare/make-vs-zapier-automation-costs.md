A low monthly price is only useful if the plan covers the workflow you need. Before choosing Make or Zapier, map the work and estimate how often each step runs.

The two services describe usage with different units. Comparing a credit allowance directly with a task allowance can lead to the wrong budget.

## Understand the documented billing units

Zapier says successful billable actions consume tasks, while triggers and a number of built-in steps do not. Its task guide also lists exceptions with different usage rates, including some AI and other products. A simple action count is therefore a starting point, not a universal billing rule. [Source: Zapier task measurement](https://help.zapier.com/hc/en-us/articles/8496196837261-How-is-task-usage-measured-in-Zapier).

Make uses credits. Its documentation explains that many non-AI operations use a fixed credit amount, while some features have different or dynamic consumption. When your workflow connects to an external AI provider, that provider's charges may also need to be budgeted separately. [Sources: Make credits](https://help.make.com/credits) and [feature credit usage](https://help.make.com/how-features-use-credits).

Check the live plan pages for the allowance, billing interval, and required features. The calculation below deliberately uses hypothetical usage rather than a price that may depend on a promotion or annual commitment. [Sources: Make pricing](https://www.make.com/en/pricing) and [Zapier pricing](https://zapier.com/pricing).

## Build a small usage worksheet

Imagine a business receives 300 form submissions each month. Every approved submission needs a customer record, a team notification, and a tracking-sheet entry.

If those three steps are each ordinary billable Zapier actions and run once per submission, the illustrative estimate is 300 × 3 = 900 tasks. It excludes retries, additional branches, and any product-specific rates. It also assumes all submissions reach those actions.

For a hypothetical Make implementation with four executions per submission, each costing one credit, the estimate is 300 × 4 = 1,200 credits. This is an arithmetic example, not a claim that Make will implement that workflow in exactly four executions. Measure the actual scenario, including triggers, searches, returned items, and other processing.

Do not compare 900 and 1,200 as if they were the same unit or a direct cost ranking. Apply each platform's current plan terms to its own measured total.

## Test normal and awkward cases

Run a small pilot with sample records. Include a duplicate submission, a missing field, and a temporary failure in a connected service. Inspect what gets created and what the usage history reports.

Decide how the workflow recognizes a record it has already processed. A repeated notification may be merely annoying, while a duplicate customer entry could create ongoing cleanup work.

Add an approval stage for actions that your business needs to review. Keep the first version small enough that another person can understand why it ran.

## Budget maintenance as well as usage

Record the time needed to repair the pilot, update a field mapping, and explain the setup to a colleague. Add any separate application or AI-service costs to the worksheet.

Choose the service that supports the required actions at an acceptable measured cost and is understandable to the person maintaining it. A dependable automation should save work after the setup is finished, not simply look inexpensive on the pricing page.
