A ten-minute voiceover begins with a timing plan. Pasting a long script into a speech generator and hoping it lands at exactly ten minutes leaves too much to chance.

Use the following workflow to produce narration in sections, correct errors, and assemble a finished recording. The word counts below are planning examples, not guarantees about any voice model.

## Start with a realistic word budget

At an assumed 140 spoken words per minute, ten minutes contains about 1,400 words. If you reserve one minute for pauses, transitions, or moments when the visuals speak for themselves, the spoken portion becomes about 1,260 words at that same pace.

The calculation is simple: spoken minutes multiplied by your chosen words per minute. Calibrate that estimate with a sample from your actual narrator. If the first 140 words take 75 seconds, revise the plan before generating the rest.

Divide the draft into an opening, several explanation sections, and a closing. Give each section a purpose. A paragraph that repeats the previous one is a better editing target than a recording that needs to be sped up.

## Prepare a script that is easy to speak

Read the text aloud once. Expand ambiguous abbreviations, decide how numbers should be spoken, and separate narration from production notes. Keep a pronunciation list for names and terms that recur.

When the model supports performance instructions, specify the intended delivery. Google documents natural-language control over [Gemini TTS](/tools/gemini-tts/) style, pace, tone, and accent. A starting direction could be: "Explain this calmly to a beginner, with a short pause after each question." Treat that as a starting prompt and listen to the result. [Source: Google TTS documentation](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/speech-generation).

## Approve a sample before generating everything

Generate the opening and one technically difficult paragraph first. Listen for missing words, mispronunciations, abrupt endings, and an unsuitable emotional tone.

[ElevenLabs](/tools/elevenlabs/) documents that voice and model selection affect the output, and that different models expose different controls. Save the exact voice, model, and supported settings used for the approved sample. [Source: ElevenLabs generation guide](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/eleven-creative/playground/text-to-speech).

Next, generate manageable sections within your chosen tool's current limits. Name them in order, such as 01-opening and 02-main-explanation. Keep the approved text beside each recording so corrections do not accidentally reintroduce an older draft.

## Assemble the narration before timing captions

Place the sections on an editing timeline and listen across every join. Adjust gaps where the performance sounds interrupted. Keep pauses that help explain an idea; remove silence only when it is accidental or distracting.

Measure the assembled duration. If the recording is too long, first shorten sentences or remove repetition. If it is short, check whether the explanation is actually complete before adding words merely to fill time.

Generate or align captions against the final audio, then check them while listening. Editing the voiceover after caption timing can create a second round of repair work.

## Finish with a full playback

Listen from beginning to end with the intended script visible. Confirm the opening, closing, difficult terms, and every section transition. Save an editable project, the approved script, and the final audio as separate files.

The useful milestone is a checked recording that fits your story. Ten minutes is a target for planning, not a reason to keep an unnecessary sentence.
